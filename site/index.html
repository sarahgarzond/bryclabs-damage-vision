          How a photo becomes evidence
          
        
            
          
          1. Ensemble. Independent vision models read the photo, each constrained to the same JSON schema with a fixed vocabulary of 13 damage types, 12 building components and 12 materials.
            2. 
          
          2. Fusion. Findings are matched across models by type and box overlap. Agreement is the share of models that report a finding.
            3. 
          
          3. Review. A separate judge model checks every merged finding against the pixels: supported, uncertain or unsupported.
            4. 
          
          4. Rules. Unsupported findings without majority agreement are removed and kept for audit. Unconfirmed or low-agreement findings are flagged uncertain.
            5. 
          
          5. Output. Schema-valid JSON with boxes, status, confidence, limitations and the information an adjuster still needs.
            6. 
        
          

        
          Definitions
          
        
          
          
          Agreement
          a(f) = (1/M) · Σm 1[f ∈ Fm], matched when types agree and IoU ≥ 0.2
          
          
          Box overlap
          IoU(A, B) = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|
          
          
          Confidence
          p = σ(β0 + βᵀx), logistic regression on agreement, judge verdict and flags, fitted on labelled photos
          
          
          Uncertainty
          CI95 = [q0.025, q0.975] over B = 1,000 bootstrap resamples of test images
          
          
          Label reliability
          κ = (po − pe) / (1 − pe), Cohen's kappa between two blind labellers
          
          
          Robustness
          J(S, S′) = |S ∩ S′| / |S ∪ S′| under blur, darkness, JPEG compression, crop, rotation, flip and low resolution
          
          
          Ranking
          AUROC = P(c⁺ > c⁻), whether correct findings receive higher confidence
          
        
          

        
          What a photo cannot establish
          
        
            
          
          • Cause, wetness, depth or hidden damage. These are listed as limitations, never as findings.
            • 
          
          • Coverage. The output is evidence for a reviewer, not a decision.
